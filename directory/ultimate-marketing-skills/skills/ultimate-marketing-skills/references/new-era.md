# How marketing works now

Read this before planning anything. The tools in UMS only help if the plan
underneath them fits how attention works in 2026. Each principle says what
changed, the evidence, and what Claude does differently because of it.
Research of 25-26 November 2026; sources in `research.md` and the notes.

## 1. The feed decides who sees it, not the follower count

Recommendation feeds now show most people posts from accounts they do not
follow. TikTok ranks on what viewers do with a video (watches, finishes,
rewatches, shares, follows, skips); X's open-sourced ranker scores each post
on predicted replies, reposts and negative signals, and discounts accounts a
viewer does not follow but does not exclude them; Instagram's Reels ranking
leans on watch time, likes and sends.

**So Claude:** treats every post as a fresh audition to strangers. A new
account can win with one good post; an old account wins nothing by being old.
It writes for the stranger who scrolls past in a second, not for fans.

## 2. Native beats repurposed

Instagram demotes reposted, watermarked or lightly edited content, and on
30 April 2026 extended its originality rules to photos and carousels. Teams
know it: HubSpot's 2026 survey found 49.4% reuse the same content across
platforms, while 39.5% tailor it per platform.

**So Claude:** keeps one idea and makes a native version per platform: its
own cut, length, caption, on-screen text and format. It never uploads a file
with another app's watermark, and never pastes the same caption everywhere.

## 3. Short video is the main event

Marketers in HubSpot's 2026 survey (1,500+) rank short-form video first for
ROI (48.6%). YouTube Shorts run up to 3 minutes, and since 31 March 2025
every start or replay counts as a view. The first two seconds decide whether
anyone sees the rest, and most feeds start muted.

**So Claude:** proposes video first for consumer apps, games, shops and local
businesses, with the payoff on screen in the first two seconds and captions
burned in. It renders, then looks at the contact sheet before calling it done.

## 4. Volume of honest attempts, then double down

Reach is heavy-tailed. In Buffer's study of 1.7M posts (March 2025), mean
engagement on X was 328 per post against a median of about 4: a few posts
do almost all of it. You cannot predict which, so you need many good tries.

**So Claude:** plans for steady output (three short videos a week is a
realistic floor for a solo founder) and a weekly read of results against the
account's own median. It repeats what landed and cuts what did not, instead of
polishing one perfect campaign.

## 5. People trust people: founders, makers, creators

LinkedIn said in March 2026 that its feed now ranks with generative
recommenders, demotes engagement bait and targets engagement pods. Founder
and maker accounts consistently outperform brand pages in practice, and paid
creators are only one channel (21.2% of brands in HubSpot's sample use
influencers). In sales, the founder sells first: Merge ran about 100
discovery calls before building and its founders handled every sales call for
eight months (self-reported).

**So Claude:** writes in the founder's own voice from things they wrote,
shows the work (builds, numbers, decisions, mistakes), and drafts from the
person, not the logo. Creator deals are always disclosed.

## 6. Distribution is built before launch

For games, wishlists come mostly from the store page and festivals, and the
lift from Steam Next Fest grows with the wishlists a game already has (median
gain of 23.7K for games starting above 100K, 462 for games under 1K;
howtomarketagame survey, February 2025). For apps and SaaS, a list and an
audience that exist before launch day decide how the launch goes.

**So Claude:** starts the store page, the email list and one channel early,
and treats launch week as the moment to cash in attention built before it.

## 7. Community and owned channels outlast any algorithm

Reddit has about 130M daily users (Q2 2026) and its threads are cited heavily
in Google and AI answers; Discord's discoverable servers get public web pages
from 16 November 2026. Email is the one channel no feed can take away.

**So Claude:** points every post at one thing people can join (a list, a
server, a wishlist), takes part in communities as a member who says what they
made, and builds the email sequences early.

## 8. Search now answers instead of linking

People clicked a result 8% of the time when Google showed an AI summary, and
15% without one (Pew, July 2025). Answer engines quote pages and threads that
say plainly what something is, what it costs and how it compares.

**So Claude:** writes the pages people and answer engines quote
(comparisons, alternatives, real how-tos, plain pricing) and gets the product
mentioned where answers come from, instead of chasing keywords.

## 9. AI makes it faster, not better: taste and proof are the edge

In the Content Marketing Institute's 2026 study (n=1,132), teams credit AI
for speed (78%) far more than quality (17%); under speed pressure 29% report
more generic content, and 52% have no policy on disclosing AI use. Generic
copy is now the default, so it is invisible.

**So Claude:** uses its speed for volume and variants, then spends the effort
on taste and proof: a specific number, a real clip, a customer's words, the
founder's own phrasing. Every post goes through the copy audit. Real product
footage beats generated footage; generated media is labelled where the
platform asks.

## 10. Trust is regulated now

Fake and AI-written reviews, bought followers and views are banned in the US
(FTC rule, 21 October 2024; up to $53,088 per violation) and the UK (since
6 April 2025). Paid posts need a clear "ad" label in the post. EU AI Act
transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026.

**So Claude:** never fakes social proof, always discloses paid and generated
content, and keeps the approval queue between every draft and the public.

## What to drop from the old playbook

- Press releases as a launch plan. Nobody reads them; journalists follow
  makers and communities.
- Hashtag walls and engagement bait ("comment YES"). Ranked down on every
  major platform.
- One master asset cross-posted everywhere with another app's watermark.
- Buying followers, likes or reviews. Illegal in the US and UK, and the
  platforms remove them.
- Brand-voice adjectives ("innovative, seamless") instead of proof.
- Measuring views and opens alone. Judge by watch time, sends, saves,
  replies, clicks and the action that matters (wishlists, installs, signups).
- Spending on ads before anything has worked organically. Boost only posts
  already in the account's top tenth.
